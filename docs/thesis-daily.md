# Daily thesis creation

Macro runs this **once** per pass. The daily pipe invokes it **now** (`run-now`) or at **8am Hong Kong**, **all seven days**. Same process. The clock is a trigger, not the work.

Standing rules apply. Read the notebook first ([`notebook.md`](notebook.md)). Read the Board ([`board-intake.md`](board-intake.md)). Intake must already have grown or the pipe has already failed.

Tonight — meaning this pass — you produce one or more of:

- a **thesis draft** for Nathan (Architect carries it)
- a **join-to-theme** note (this print sits on a kept thesis)
- a **written duplicate-stop**
- a **sit** (nothing here is a judgement today)

You do not produce a live thesis, an Investment Idea, or a name. A keep later may open ideas; a keep is not names.

A draft is not a thesis until Nathan keeps it. Macro does not mint live.

---

## The stop that ends the day

Keep drafting only while a new card would be a **new judgement**.

Stop when Macro can write this sentence and stand behind it:

> Another card would only duplicate a narrative already on the Board or in a live thesis.

That stop is a **written call**, not a quiet halt. Put it in the day’s notes and in the notebook.

If you cannot write that sentence, you are not done — unless you are sitting because the gates failed (no judgement, still a fight, notebook stop). Sitting with a reason is also a written call.

---

## The process (same order every morning)

### Step 0 — Notebook first

On the first-ever pass, the notebook may be empty. Say so.

On every later pass, read, in Nathan’s words:

- what he kept, and why
- what he dropped, and why
- what he parked or sat on
- any sentence of his that says “this is not a thesis” or “this sits on TH-__”

Do not paraphrase his reason into a smarter reason. If you need to recall a drop, quote him.

If today’s Board is only reprinting a situation he already dropped, and nothing new answers **his** reason, stop. Write sit, with the notebook line cited.

If today’s Board is only reprinting a situation he already kept, do not draft a cousin. Join the print to the kept theme.

### Step 1 — Take the candidate list from intake

You already marked jobs on the Board. Work only the **candidate judgement** pile.

Fights stay fights. Stories stay stories. Neighbors stay neighbors.

### Step 2 — Grab only if needed

Grab means: go get a dated fact that would change whether you can write one judgement, name what would make it false, or honestly say already-in-the-price / not checked.

You may grab when the judgement is almost writable and one dated fact is missing.

You may not grab:

- a tour of the daily tape pack “in case something is there”
- a name, a ticker, or a company story
- a second story to rescue a weak call
- enough tape to retrofit a move that already happened

If after a short grab you still cannot write one judgement, sit. The Board stays the Board.

This step is not the daily tape pack. If you need that pack, you are in the other file.

### Step 3 — Write one judgement, in plain language

One call. Forward-looking. A sentence a stranger can argue with.

Required on the draft:

- **The judgement** — the call, not the mechanism, not the fact, not how something works
- **What it is not** — the obvious wrong reading
- **From the Board** — which items feed it, and why they are the same call
- **Left on the Board** — which items stay stories, fights, neighbors, or prints on another theme
- **Clock** — tag: `now`, `6–12 months`, `next year`, or a dated window
- **What would make this false** — a world print
- **What is not a reason to fold** — the noise that will try
- **Already in the price, or not checked**

If you cannot write “what it is not,” you have a mood. Stop.

Do not collage leftover stories onto the card.

Do not collapse two situations into one sentence.

No ticker. No Investment Idea. No name.

### Step 4 — Board-measure

Measure the draft against the **rest of the Board**. Every remaining item gets one of four marks:

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Strengthens** | This Board item makes the call more believable. It may sit as evidence. It does not become a second thesis. |
| **Weakens** | This Board item is a real objection. It goes in the objection paragraph. It does not silently vanish. |
| **Different flavor** | Same neighborhood, not the same call. Leave it on the Board. Do not bolt it on. |
| **Leave it alone** | Not about this call. Do not drag it onto the card. |

Write the marks. A draft that skipped this step is unfinished.

### Step 5 — Tension with every live thesis

For **each** live thesis, write a tension note on the new card:

> We notice the tension with TH-__. They may or may not contradict. We think ____.

That note is the point of synthesis. Do not wait for the ideas step to notice a fight.

Do not pick a winner here. Do not fold one thesis to make the new card tidy. Do not write a mash-up judgement that assumes both stay true.

**Pattern (method only, 15 August 2026).** One live call: they keep building, and the plants stay scarce. Another live call: the circular funding of that build will not hold. Those two can both be right. They can also meet in a blow-up that starves the buildings and forces a look at the first call. The tension note says that. It does not choose.

If there are no live theses yet, write “no live thesis to tension against.”

### Step 6 — Offer to Architect, never auto-keep

Give Architect the draft in the shape below. Then stop.

Do not assign a live `TH-*` id. Do not move it onto Theses as kept. Do not write an Investment Idea. Do not spawn a name.

Architect talks to Nathan. Nathan keeps, folds, parks, or sits.

If Nathan does not speak, the draft stays a draft. Silence is not a keep.

### Step 7 — After Nathan answers

Architect tells Macro what Nathan said, in Nathan’s words.

| Nathan’s answer | Macro does next |
| --- | --- |
| **Keep** | Architect puts it on the local Theses column with a clock tag. Macro runs [`investment-ideas.md`](investment-ideas.md), then [`notebook.md`](notebook.md). Macro now owns [`refine.md`](refine.md) on that card. |
| **Fold** | Notebook only. Do not write ideas. Do not “keep it warm” as a half-thesis. |
| **Park / sit** | Notebook only. You may offer again only if the Board changed. |
| **Join to a theme he already kept** | Sit the print on that `TH-*`. Notebook. No new id. |
| **No answer** | Draft stays a draft. Notebook: he did not speak. |

Then, if you can write the duplicate-stop sentence, write it and end the morning. If you cannot, take the next candidate judgement from step 1 and repeat from step 3. One morning, one process, as many drafts as are actually new — then a written stop.

## What a draft writes (offer shape)

Ordinary sentences.

- **Judgement**
- **What it is not**
- **From the Board** (ids and why they are the same call)
- **Left on the Board** (and why)
- **Board-measure** (strengthens / weakens / different flavor / leave it alone)
- **Tension notes** (one per live thesis)
- **Clock** (tag plus the testable window in words)
- **What would make this false**
- **What is not a reason to fold**
- **Already in the price, or not checked**
- **Objection** — the strongest honest case against, in one paragraph
- **Notebook** — any prior keep/fold this draft is answering
- **Asked of Nathan** — keep / fold / park / sit / join to an existing theme

### Sit (also a real output)

- why nothing was a judgement today
- which prints were joined to a kept theme instead of drafted

### Join-to-theme note (not a new draft)

- the kept theme, by the id Nathan already kept
- the Board item that agrees
- the sentence: this sits on the theme; it is not a new card for its own sake

### Duplicate-stop (written call)

- the sentence in “The stop that ends the day”
- which Board items and live theses would be duplicated
- date and time (Hong Kong)

---

## Failure modes specific to this pass

- Starting from this morning’s tape and “finding” a thesis. That is the other file, or worse.
- Offering two clocks as two theses.
- Filling “already in the price” with a vibe so the draft looks finished.
- Writing the Investment Idea because the draft felt strong.
- Quietly stopping when another judgement was still sitting on the Board.
- Writing a mash-up so TH-01 and TH-02 do not have to tension.
