# Fundamental + momentum

Working spec for the Micro / candidate desk. Nathan edits this file directly.

This is a design document. It does not change the live board.

---

## Bring this back

A short version the Architect can say in ordinary language. The rest of the file is the working spec.

Macro already wrote the theme. This desk does not write another one. A story is still not a thesis.

Once a theme exists, the desk looks at how the world is moving against that theme. Later — only later — it looks at names.

Every live item needs two views. The fundamental view: what has to stay true in the world, and later in the business. The momentum view: is the tape agreeing, or is the picture early, or already spent. Both are required. Neither is enough alone.

A good picture with a dead tape is a wait, not a bet. A moving tape with no picture is not a bet either.

A name is only a way to hold the theme. If the reason you picked it breaks, you leave. You do not write a new reason to stay. A famous company at a price that already assumes everything goes right is not a bet. Sit is a real answer.

- **[ours]** — our construction. Most of the desk procedure is this mark, and that is fine as long as it is not dressed up as theirs.

On a live card, none of these names appear. The method is used. The people are not cited. A card that says "invert" as a slogan, or name-drops a manager, is wrong even if the thought was right.

Two standing prohibitions:

1. We do not have the 272-line chart list. Do not build one and call it his. The tape work below is the series that would move if *this* theme is true. **[sourced · FXLM]** for the habit and the windows. **[ours]** for every series this desk actually watches.
2. Do not build this desk around "money is easy / money is tight." Liquidity can be a fact you read, with a date. It cannot be the personality of the bot, and it cannot be the frame that every card is forced through.

This document does not assert what markets are doing today. Examples are patterns, or the house's already-kept theme used as a walk-through. If the desk wants to claim something is live, it reads the series or the filing and says so with the date.

## 1. What this desk is

This desk starts when a theme already exists.

It does not mint theses. That is Macro. If this desk writes a `TH-*` card, that is a bug, not a shortcut.

It does not jump to a ticker until the theme is clear. A name with no parent theme is a thrown object, not a candidate. Thrown names can be screened later; they cannot invent the theme they needed.

**This desk can conclude:**

- whether the world is still moving in the way the live theme requires
- whether that motion is early, confirmed, already spent, or broken
- an expression — the *shape* of how the theme would be held, still not a ticker
- later, whether a name is an honest vehicle for that expression
- a clock and a kill that the name actually inherits, or a written reason it does not
- sit, wait on tape, die, or die as a hold

**This desk cannot conclude:**

- a new world picture, a new theme, or a replacement theme when the first one breaks
- that a company story is an investment **[sourced · Munger]**
- a size, a strike, or an order. Those are later, on Action / Book, and only after both views have already passed
- a live market fact it has not read, with the date

Mapping to the existing funnel (confirm or correct this, Nathan): Macro writes and maintains `board` cards and the `theses_*` claims. This desk begins at `thesis_candidates` (the expression), then `stocks`, then `screen`. `monitor` is where both views have been written as conditions and neither has printed yet. The `skew` / `priced` seam is shared: Macro says what is already priced at the world level; this desk says whether a given expression or name still has room.

The kept theme on the board — "the bottleneck, not the next model" / TH-01 — is the house example of a theme this desk is allowed to work. It is not a second theme, and this spec does not mint one next to it.

## 2. The mix

This is the whole method. The rest of the file is how to run it.

### 2.1 Two views, both required

**Fundamental view.** What has to stay true. In the world, that is the theme's variable — the thing the thesis dies without. In a business, that is how the money is made, and what the customer cannot leave. **[sourced · Munger]** for the business half. **[ours]** for stating it as "what has to stay true."

**Momentum view.** Is the tape agreeing, or is the picture early, or already spent. The tape is price and related series, in more than one window. It is not a headline, and it is not a vibe. **[sourced · FXLM]**

Neither view is enough alone. A true picture with a dead tape is not a bet. A rising tape with no picture is not a bet. The desk that only writes one of the two has not finished.

### 2.2 Two orders, both legitimate

About 75–80 percent of ideas start from the fundamental, and the tape confirms or vetoes. If the picture is good and the tape stinks, do not do it. Sometimes the chart moves first, and then the story is found. **[sourced · FXLM]**

Both directions are allowed. They have different failure modes, so the desk labels which one it used.

- **Fundamental-first** fails by being early and stubborn. You sit on a true picture while the tape says no, and you talk yourself into "the market is wrong" as if that were a position.
- **Tape-first** fails by retrofitting a story onto a move that already happened. A tape-first idea is held to a harder kill, because the easy cheat is to discover a reason after the price has already gone.

A tape-first idea that cannot find a story does not become a name. It is a note that something moved. Macro can look at it. This desk does not mint a theme to explain a chart.

### 2.3 The veto

The veto is not advisory. Good picture, bad tape: do not do it. **[sourced · FXLM]**

The picture is still written. It sits as waiting on tape, with the tape condition named. That is how the existing waitlists already work: they do not arm because the picture is true. They arm only if the price condition and the world condition both print.

The other veto is the inverse, and it is **[ours]** as a pairing, built from the same night: a tape that looks good while the variable has already broken is not a late entry. It is a name you are about to invent a second reason for. See 5.4.

### 2.4 What "the tape" means here

Not two hundred charts. Not a secret list.

Once a theme exists, the tape is the small set of series that would have to move — or refuse to move — if that theme is true. Groups, a commodity, a curve, a currency, a credit spread, later a name. Whatever the theme actually implies.

Every series is looked at in three windows: daily, weekly, monthly, each roughly 8–20 bars. **[sourced · FXLM]** ("roughly" is his. Do not harden it into an exact bar count.)

- **Daily (~8–20 days)** — is something happening now.
- **Weekly (~8–20 weeks)** — is it a move or a wiggle.
- **Monthly (~8–20 months)** — is the level somewhere unusual, and has this thing been still while its drivers moved.

A disagreement between windows is itself information. Daily up against monthly down is a rally in a downtrend, not a turn, until the weekly agrees.

The desk does not need Macro's full pack. It needs the series the live theme named, plus the group the expression would sit in. If the theme cannot name what to watch, the theme is not clear enough to work, and that is Macro's problem to fix.

### 2.5 What "fundamental" means here

At the world step, the fundamental is the theme's variable and its kill. TH-01 dies on utilization down, rents and memory down, large capex cancelled. A second share-price scare without those prints is not a kill. That is already written. This desk does not rewrite it.

At the name step, the fundamental is the business. How it makes money. What the customer cannot leave. What would make you look stupid for owning it. Whether the price already assumes the good case. **[sourced · Munger]**

Liquidity, if it matters, is read as a fact: issuance, a spread, a facility, a dated print. It is not a mood, and it is not a slogan.

## 3. Once a theme exists: how the world is moving

This step happens before any ticker. If you are already looking at a name, you skipped it.

### 3.1 Take the theme as given

Read the live claim, the force, the clock, and the kill. If those are missing, sit. Do not fill them in from a podcast. Do not sharpen a vague theme by picking a stock you like.

A theme that is still an observation or a fight on the board is not a theme this desk can work. Card 11 on the live board — power is the problem, nobody agrees what happens next — is a fight. It is not a parent.

### 3.2 Name the variable

What has to stay true. One sentence. If you need three variables, you do not have a theme, you have a tour.

The variable is what you will flip on. When it breaks, the desk is done with this theme. You do not keep the expression. You do not keep the names. You do not write a cousin claim to stay in the same names. **[sourced · SD]** for concentrating on the factor that actually moves the thing; **[ours]** for calling it "the variable" and making the flip a hard stop.

### 3.3 Read the world against that variable

Look at the series the theme implies, in the three windows. Words, not scores.

Ask only this: if the claim is true, what should already be visible in the world, and is it?

Four states. Pick one. "A bit of both" is not a state.

- **Confirmed.** The world prints still match the claim, and the tape of the relevant groups is not fighting it.
- **Early.** The claim still looks true, and the tape has not agreed yet. This is a wait. It is the most common honest state.
- **Spent.** The tape already ran, or the price of the obvious hold already assumes the good case. The picture can still be true and not be a bet.
- **Broken.** The variable printed the kill. The desk stops. It reports the kill upward. It does not mint the opposite.

Sitting on early is correct. Acting on spent is how a true theme becomes a crowded name. Acting on broken is a bug.

July 2026 on TH-01 is the house pattern for *reading both*: the shares panicked and the factories did not. That is a tape event against a world claim, not a new theme. **[ours]** as a reading of the existing board, not a market call in this document.

### 3.4 The expression comes from this pass

If the state is confirmed or early, you may write the shape of the hold. Still not a ticker.

The vehicle is not the idea. **[sourced · SD]** He could sit in bonds, currencies, commodities, or equities, and he was willing to have nothing in a class where he had no idea. The same discipline here: the theme is the idea. The expression is one way to hold it. If the honest hold is not an equity, this desk says so and does not force a stock row.

If the expression cannot be stated without naming a company, the expression is not clear yet. That is this desk's problem to fix, not a reason to skip ahead.

## 4. The expression (thesis candidate)

An expression is a `thesis_candidate`. It is a shape. It is not a stock, a buy, or a price. The live board already says this on CA-01. Keep saying it.

### 4.1 What it has to name

- the parent theme, by id
- the shape of the hold, in ordinary language
- what it is not — the obvious wrong vehicle, written down, because that is where thrown names will try to sneak in
- the variable it inherits, and the kill
- why it is unfinished, if it is
- whether several future names would be one factor bet rather than several bets

### 4.2 Invert the expression before any name

Ask how this *way of holding* fails even if the theme is true. **[sourced · Munger]**

The theme can be right and the expression still be stupid. Long the default foundry name can be the crowded side of a true bottleneck. Long a US-listed humanoid can be the expensive side of a true input squeeze. Those are not theme kills. They are expression kills. The live skew lines on TH-01 and TH-02 are already doing this job. This desk makes it a required step, not a field you fill if you remember.

If inversion says the obvious hold has no room, you do not spawn that name "so the column is not empty." You write the expression as unfinished, or you look for a less obvious vehicle, or you sit.

### 4.3 Concentrate when the conviction is real

When the expression is the one you actually believe, you do not also keep four cousins so the desk looks busy. Diversification of the same factor is not safety. **[sourced · FXLM]** / **[sourced · SD]**

The opposite is also true. No conviction: no expression. A weak theme does not get a candidate card to "keep it warm."

## 5. Then names

Only after sections 3 and 4. A ticker before a clear theme is how the desk starts working backwards from a company it already likes.

### 5.1 Gates before a ticker is written

All four, or the name stays unwritten.

1. The parent theme is live and clear.
2. The world state against that theme is confirmed or early — not broken, and not spent unless you are explicitly writing a wait for a drawdown.
3. The expression is written, including what it is not.
4. This name is a vehicle for *that* expression. If you need a new sentence that is not the expression, you do not have a vehicle. You have a new story.

A thrown name that fails gate 4 dies, or dies as a hold. It does not get a new parent invented for it. The live board already did this: a spender is not the scarce plant; a test-side socket is not the scarce plant. Those were not new theses. They were failed joins.

### 5.2 The name, fundamental view

Ordinary sentences. No score in place of the sentences. A later thinking number, if Nathan still wants one, is a summary of this writing, not a substitute.

**How it makes money.** The cash has to come from a customer, in a way you can say without a slide. If you cannot say it, you are outside the circle and the name is too hard. **[sourced · Munger]**

**What the customer cannot leave.** Not "they have a brand." The switching cost, the scarce slot, the habit, the spec that only this plant can hit. If the customer can leave next year without pain, you do not have a business you understand. You have a cycle. A cycle can still be a vehicle for a theme. It is not a great business. Say which one you think you have.

**Invert.** How this name makes you look stupid even if the theme is true. Debt, a customer who is also a competitor, a single plant, a price that only works if the good case arrives on time, a kill that is not the theme's kill. Write the ugly case first. **[sourced · Munger]**

**Circle.** Do we actually understand this, or are we repeating a podcast. Too hard is a legal disposition. Unfinished is honest. A fake screen is not.

**Price and the good case.** A great business at a price that already assumes the good case is not a bet. **[sourced · Munger]** for refusing to confuse quality with a purchase; **[ours]** for that sentence. The question is not "is this a wonderful company." The question is whether the wonderful outcome is already the asking price.

**Opportunity cost.** The next best use of the same desk time and the same capital is the other vehicle for the *same* theme, or sitting. It is not "this is cheaper than last year." **[sourced · Munger]**

**A company story is not an investment.** A lab, a founder, a product launch, a patriotic assembly story — those can be true and still not be a way to hold the theme. **[sourced · Munger]**

Avoiding the stupid case is the job. Brilliance is not required and is usually a warning. **[sourced · Munger]**

### 5.3 The name, momentum view

Same three windows as the world step, now on the name and on its group.

- Is the name agreeing with the expression, or is it the default crowded ticker whose move already happened.
- Is the name doing the same thing as its group, or something else. Something else needs a sentence. "It's a bit special" is not a sentence.
- If the news is fine and the name is not acting well, believe the name. **[sourced · FXLM]**
- Early / confirmed / spent / fighting — pick one, same as 3.3. Fighting the parent tape is a reason to wait or to die, not a reason to write a new story about why this name is different.

Valuation does not time the entry. The tape and the world prints do. Valuation tells you how far a thing *can* go once the variable is in motion, and whether the good case is already paid for. **[sourced · SD]**

### 5.4 Do not invent a second thesis to keep a name

If the join fails, the name dies. If the parent variable breaks, the name dies with the theme.

The failure this rule exists to stop: you liked the name, the original reason went away, and you found a new reason so you would not have to look wrong. That is how a bottleneck desk ends up holding a software company, a spender, or a consumable, and calling it the same idea.

A cousin fact can be written as an observation and handed back to Macro. It cannot keep the name alive on this desk.

The Indonesian episode is the historical warning: a second story ("this one is different") after the first trade worked. **[sourced · FXLM]** We use the lesson. We do not retell the biography on a card.

### 5.5 Flip when the variable breaks

No mechanical price stop as a personality. Conditions. **[sourced · FXLM]**

The condition was named when the expression was written. When it prints, you are out. You do not wait for the annual report to agree. You do not average down to "get even." You do not flip into the opposite name-level bet unless Macro has already written the opposite theme. This desk does not mint that opposite.

### 5.6 Concentrate, again, at the name

One or two names that actually carry the expression. Not a basket that is the same factor eight times. If ST-01 and the power landlords and the memory name are one bet, say they are one bet. The live unfinished cards already worry about this. Keep the worry.

Conviction is real or it is not. When it is, the desk is allowed to be large later. When it is not, the desk is small or empty. Empty is cleaner than eight half-reasons. **[sourced · FXLM]**

## 6. The pass

Same order every time this desk is run. The early steps are the ones people skip in order to get to a ticker, so they are numbered.

1. **Receive the theme.** Live claim, force, clock, kill. If none, or if the claim is still a fight, sit. Do not mint.
2. **Name the variable.** One sentence. What has to stay true.
3. **Read the world tape** against that variable, three windows, only the series the theme implies.
4. **State the mix.** Confirmed / early / spent / broken. Label fundamental-first or tape-first.
5. **Broken:** report the kill. Stop. No replacement theme. No rescue name.
6. **Spent:** write that the good case is already in the obvious hold. Do not spawn the obvious name to "have something on the desk."
7. **Early:** write the wait. Name the tape condition. Do not arm.
8. **Confirmed or early-with-a-named-wait:** write or refresh the expression. Invert it. Still no ticker.
9. **Only then** spawn a name, or accept a thrown name through the four gates.
10. **Name pass.** Fundamental view and momentum view, both written, order labeled. How it fails. Whether we understand it. Price vs the good case. The other vehicle, or sit.
11. **Disposition.** Screen, waitlist, die, or die as a hold. If it dies, the reason is the failed join or the broken variable — not a new theme.

Sitting is a real output. A blank pass that skipped the world step to stare at a ticker is not sitting. It is a skipped pass.

## 7. What a card writes

No manager names. No slogans. Ordinary sentences.

**On an expression (`thesis_candidate`):**

- parent theme
- the shape of the hold, and what it is not
- the variable inherited
- world state against that variable: confirmed / early / spent / broken
- which order was used (fundamental-first or tape-first)
- the tape condition, if waiting
- how this hold fails even if the theme is true
- why unfinished, if it is
- whether future names are one factor

**On a name (`stock` then `screen`):**

- parent expression and parent theme
- how it makes money
- what the customer cannot leave, or an honest "this is a cycle, not a franchise we understand"
- how this name fails even if the theme is true
- whether we actually understand it, or it is too hard
- priced: does the asking price already assume the good case
- tape: early / confirmed / spent / fighting, with the windows in words
- clock and kill — inherited, or a written reason they do not transfer
- the other vehicle for the same theme, or sit
- disposition

`monitor` is not a mood. It is both conditions written in advance: a world print that must stay true, and a tape print that must happen. One condition is not enough. That is already the house rule on WL-01 / WL-02. This spec does not change it. It says why both exist: one is the fundamental view, one is the momentum view.

## 8. Against fake precision

- No composite "conviction score" that replaces the two views. If a thinking number stays on Screen, it is a label after the writing, not a substitute for it.
- No numeric tape trigger unless Nathan sets it. Not "down 12 percent in six sessions."
- No claimed chart count. The watch list is the theme's implied series. It stays short on purpose.
- No asserting a current market fact without a read and a date.
- Words for state — confirmed, early, spent, broken, fighting, unclear. "Unclear" is allowed and often correct.
- Where a number is genuinely countable — a rent, a utilization print, a dated contract, a multiple against a named good case — use it. That is the opposite of this failure.

## 9. Known failure modes of this design

Written down so they can be checked for rather than discovered.

- Minting a theme because a name was interesting. Guard: step 1, and a `TH-*` from this desk is a bug.
- Jumping to a ticker before the theme is clear. Guard: the four gates in 5.1.
- Writing only one of the two views. Guard: 2.1, and monitor requires both conditions.
- Ignoring the veto. Good picture, bad tape, still spawned. Guard: 2.3.
- Retrofitting a story onto a move. Guard: tape-first is labeled and gets a harder kill.
- Inventing a second thesis to keep a name. Guard: 5.4. Failed join dies. Broken variable dies. Cousin facts go back to Macro as observations.
- Treating a great company as a bet because it is great. Guard: 5.2, price vs the good case.
- Eight names that are one factor. Guard: 4.3 and 5.6.
- Rebuilding a 272-line list and calling it his. Guard: 2.4.
- Letting "liquidity" become the bot's personality. Guard: section 0, prohibition 2.
- Name-dropping on a card. Guard: section 0, and section 7.

## 11. Pattern walk (not a live pass)

The kept theme, used only to show the method. Not a market claim in this document. Not a board edit.

**Theme (already written, Macro's).** The plants are still the scarce thing. July's scare was in the shares. Kill: utilization, rents, memory, cancelled capex.

**Variable.** The physical thing stays scarce.

**World mix, as a pattern.** Fundamental-first: the plants were not built fast enough. Tape: you would then look at whether the groups that should reflect scarcity are agreeing, or whether they already ran, or whether they are still fighting the claim the way they did in July. You would not look at a software name to answer this.

**Expression.** Sit with whoever owns the scarce physical thing. Not the next model. Not the spender. Invert: the default foundry name may already have no room even if the plants are scarce.

**A name that joins.** It has to be a vehicle for that expression. How it makes money has to be the scarce slot, not a story about intelligence. The customer cannot leave *that slot*, or you are in a cycle and you say so. The tape of the name is read against the theme, not against a new story. If you need a drawdown and the prints still tight, that is both views, written as a wait.

**A name that does not join.** A spender. A test-side consumable. A famous franchise whose price already assumes the good case. These die, or die as a hold. They do not get a new theme.

**If the variable breaks.** Utilization down, rents and memory down, capex cancelled. The expression dies. The names die. You do not keep the famous one because it is a good company. You do not write "then it becomes a software compounder" to stay.
