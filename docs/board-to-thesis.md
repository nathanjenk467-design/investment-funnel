Working spec for Macro’s conversion of a cooked Board into an investment-theme thesis draft. Nathan edits this file directly.

Sibling specs, if present: `docs/macro-daily-pack.md` (the daily tape look) and `docs/fundamental-momentum.md` (the Micro name screen). This file does not redo either. It is only Board → thesis-draft.

## 0. How to read the provenance marks

Four marks are used throughout. Keep them on anything you add.

- **[sourced]** — a method taken from real macro-process writing (how a serious shop forms, tests, or drops a view). The mark may name the *method*, not a personality, when the method is used on a card. People are not name-dropped on drafts or live cards.
- **[shop]** — a rule Nathan or the Architect already set for this funnel. Not research. Do not dress it up as a famous shop’s rule.
- **[ours]** — this playbook’s construction. Most of the conversion procedure is this mark, and that is fine as long as it is not dressed up as theirs.
- **[open]** — a question for Nathan. Not a rule until he answers.

On a live card or a draft card, none of the research names appear. The method is used. The people are not cited. A card that says “invert” as a slogan, or name-drops a manager, is wrong even if the thought was right. **[shop]**

Two standing prohibitions:

1. Do not copy a Narrative card and rename it a thesis. **[shop]**
2. Do not frame a draft as “money is easy” or “money is tight.” Liquidity can be a dated fact already on the Board. It cannot be the personality of this conversion, and it cannot be the sentence every draft is forced through. **[shop]**

This document does not assert what markets are doing today. Examples are patterns, or the house’s already-kept walk used to show the method. If Macro wants to claim something is live, it reads the Board (and, only if needed, a grabbed fact) and says so with the date.

The Architect’s hypothesis — seeing skills plus writing gates; one situation; invert; priced or not checked; clock as a tag; correlation without a situation mints junk — is treated as a starting claim. This file keeps it where the house walk and the research agree, extends it where a serious shop actually writes more than it “sees,” and marks the rest **[open]**.

## 1. What this conversion is

Macro already has a cooked Board: kept situations, open calls, observations, housed stories, and fresh prints that update a situation already there.

This conversion turns that Board into **zero or more thesis drafts** for Nathan to keep, drop, park, or sit on.

**Macro can conclude, as a draft:**

- that several Board items are one situation, named in ordinary sentences
- that a print or a call belongs on a theme already kept, and is not a new theme
- that a Board item is still a story, a fight, or an observation, and should stay on the Board
- a clock tag, a kill, and a priced line (or an honest “not checked”)
- sit: nothing here is a thesis tonight

**Macro cannot conclude:**

- that a draft is live. Nathan keeps. Auto-keep is a bug. **[shop]**
- a ticker, an instrument, a strike, a size. Those are later, and they are not this file. **[shop]**
- a new Board card minted only because a print agreed with a situation already there. **[shop]**
- a hold (`thesis_candidate`). A hold is how you sit with a *kept* thesis. This conversion stops at the draft. **[shop]**
- a live market fact it has not read, with the date

Funnel columns this file talks about: **Board**, **Theses**, **Thesis candidates** (the hold). Names are a later tab. Clock is a tag on the thesis (`now`, `6–12 months`, or whatever was written), not its own Funnel column. **[shop]**

The live walk still shows “Theses 6–12 months” and “Theses now” as two columns. This spec does not edit that page. It writes as if clock is a tag. Whether the live columns should collapse is **[open]**.

Mapping to the rest of the funnel (confirm or correct this, Nathan): Macro cooks and maintains the Board, and it *drafts* Theses. Nathan keeps Theses. Micro begins after a thesis is kept, at the hold, then names. **[open]** on the exact hand-off; **[shop]** that this file does not write the hold.

## 2. What a thesis is vs a story vs a Board card vs a hold

A Macro bot that cannot tell these apart will mint junk. Read this section before any draft.

### 2.1 A Board card

A cooked item on the world picture. It can be a kept situation, an open call, an observation, a housed story, or a fresh print that updates something already there.

It is true enough to stay on the Board. That is not the same as being a thesis. Most Board cards will never become a thesis, and that is a working Board, not a failed conversion. **[shop]**

### 2.2 A story

A housed account of what happened, what someone said, or how a situation looks from one seat.

A story can be true, vivid, and still not be a thesis. It has no duty to name a kill, a clock, or what is already in the price. It can sit on the Board forever as a story. Copying it into the Theses column and changing the label is the Narrative-card failure. **[shop]**

### 2.3 A thesis

A forward picture Nathan kept: **one situation** that is supposed to stay true over a named clock, with a named kill, and a priced line (or “not checked”).

It is not a collage of linked nodes. It is not a headline. It is not “the world is doing AI.” It is a claim you can be wrong about in public.

A draft uses the same shape. Until Nathan keeps it, it is a draft. It does not get a live `TH-*` id as if it were already kept. **[shop]**

A now-tagged thesis is already moving the world. A 6–12 month thesis is a forward picture, not a claim that the world is already dominated by it. Those are clocks, not two kinds of truth. **[shop]** / **[ours]**

### 2.4 A hold

A thesis candidate: the *shape* of how to sit with a kept thesis. Not a name. Not a buy. Not a ticker.

This conversion does not write holds. Writing a hold because the draft felt strong is jumping the funnel. **[shop]**

### 2.5 The one-sentence test

If you cannot finish this sentence, you do not have a thesis, you have a tour:

> “The situation is ____. It stays true until ____. We will know by ____. The world already assumes ____, or we have not checked.”

If you need “and also” more than once, you probably have two situations. Stop and split, or stop and sit. **[ours]**

## 3. The skill set

Nathan named four seeing skills. A serious shop also writes with gates, changes its mind on a named kill, and refuses to mint from correlation alone. This section is the skill set. Section 4 is the order you run it.

### 3.1 How you see

These four are how a draft gets *noticed*. They are not a reason to keep.

#### Cross-disciplinary association

When a Board item is real, other fields have to move with it — not as a mood, as a mechanism.

Ask: if this situation is true, which *other* domain is forced to update? Power if the scarce thing is a plant that needs electricity. An unglamorous input if the scarce thing is a machine that cannot be built without it. A payment rail if the thing that spends has no way to pay. **[ours]** for the questions. The habit — look across fields instead of staring at the card you already like — is how a top-down shop actually works. **[sourced]**

The test is mechanical, not literary. A person who only knew field A would be forced to change what they believe about field B. If not, the cards are neighbors. Neighbors can sit next to each other on the Board. They are not one thesis.

Association that produces a catchy title and no shared kill is a leap. Discard it. **[shop]**

#### Resonance with existing world knowledge and the knowledge base

Before you invent, look up.

The knowledge base, in this shop, is three layers, read in this order: **[shop]** / **[ours]**

1. Nathan’s notebook — what he already kept or dropped, in his words.
2. The cooked Board and the live theses — situations already housed, calls already open, prints already sitting on a theme.
3. World knowledge — historical analogs and ordinary facts about how a mechanism works, used only when they share the *hold*, the *cost*, and the *kill shape*, not the headline.

Resonance is “we have already seen this *mechanism*.” It is not “this feels like a year people talk about.” An analog that only matches the newspaper word is decoration. An analog that matches a price held by a finite buffer, or a scare in the shares while the physical thing did not move, is usable — as a check on the situation, not as a claim that the same ending is due. **[sourced]** for analog-as-mechanism; **[ours]** for refusing headline-rhyme.

If the notebook already dropped this situation, you do not re-draft it because the wording is fresher. You only come back if the Board has a new fact that answers *his* reason for dropping it. **[shop]**

#### Correlation with other theses, narratives, and trends

Correlation is a check. It is not a mint.

For every candidate situation, ask: does this already live inside a kept thesis, a dropped thesis, a housed story, or a trend already on the Board?

- If a print *agrees* with a kept theme, it sits on that theme. It does not become a new card for its own sake. **[shop]**
- If a call is evidence for a theme (same force, same kill window), it is evidence. It is not a second thesis. The house walk already did this: an open call can sit next to a theme without minting a cousin. **[shop]**
- If two cards move together in the tape but do not share a force and a kill, that is correlation without a situation. That is the failure mode that mints junk. Stop. **[shop]** / **[ours]**

A serious shop asks whether the world already agrees, because agreement is usually already in the price. **[sourced]** Correlation with a popular narrative is a warning, not a boost.

#### Linking nodes into one situation

Several Board cards *can* feed one theme. That is the good version of “create a narrative that links multiple nodes.”

The narrative is the situation sentence. Nodes may join only if they share **the same force** and **the same kill**. If card A dies on one print and card B dies on a different print, they are two situations. Write two drafts, or write none. Do not write a medley. **[shop]** / **[ours]**

The house pattern (method only, not a live pass): four Board cards about plants still scarce, a scare that was in the shares, compute staying dear, and capital following the physical thing, can be one situation. A card about ordinary funding being exhausted is a neighbor, not a fourth theme, until it shares that kill. A fight about what happens next with power is a fight, not a parent. A payments-rail card is its own situation or it is nothing. **[shop]** as a reading of the existing walk.

“Create narratives that link nodes” must not become catchy titles or leaps. If the title is doing the joining, the situation is not joined. **[shop]**

### 3.2 How you write

Seeing without gates produces slogans. These gates are how a good shop *writes*. The Architect’s list holds. This section extends it.

#### One situation, not a collage

Write the force in one sentence. If you cannot, stop. A collage of true cards is still a collage. **[shop]**

Research agrees on the same gate under another name: a thesis has a primary driver. Supporting facts may layer on it. They do not replace it. A single driver with no kill is a story. Several drivers with no shared kill are a tour. **[sourced]**

#### Invert what would kill it

Before the draft is offered, write the ugly case. What print, in the world, would make this situation false? Not a price wiggle. A world fact. **[sourced]**

Also write what is *not* a kill — the scare that already happened in the shares, the podcast, the cousin headline — so a later pass cannot “kill” the thesis with noise. The house walk already does this. Keep doing it. **[shop]**

If you cannot name a kill, you do not understand the situation well enough to draft it. Sit. **[sourced]** / **[ours]**

Inversion is a writing gate, not a personality. On the card it reads as “this dies if ____.” It does not read as a manager’s name.

#### What is already in the price — or say not checked

The present is already in the price. A draft that only restates what everyone can already see, and then forgets to say so, is a story with a thesis label. **[sourced]**

Write one of these, in ordinary sentences:

- what the world already assumes, and where there might still be room, **or**
- **not checked** — and then the draft cannot pretend it has skew.

“Not checked” is an honest line. It is how the house already kept a theme off the desk. Filling a priced line with a vibe so the draft looks finished is a bug. **[shop]**

This conversion does not run the daily tape pack. If priced cannot be checked from the Board and a short grab, write “not checked.” Do not wander the tape until you find a chart that blesses the wording. **[shop]**

#### A clock, as a tag

Every draft gets a clock tag: `now`, `6–12 months`, or whatever was actually written (a dated call window is better than a mood). **[shop]**

The clock is when the situation is supposed to be testable, or when it is already dominating. It is not a Funnel column. It is not a reason to mint two theses that are the same situation at two speeds.

A clock with no kill is a calendar entry. A kill with no clock is a grudge. You need both, or you sit. **[ours]**

A shop that looks 18–24 months ahead is using the same gate — do not invest in the present — at a longer default. This house writes the clock it means. Do not force 18–24 onto a draft that is a Christmas call or a now-picture. **[sourced]** for the present-is-priced habit; **[shop]** for the tag.

#### Concentration

Few live theses. Sitting is a real output. A Board full of cards does not obligate a Theses column full of drafts. **[sourced]** / **[shop]**

If two drafts are the same factor in different clothes, they are one draft. If you are writing a third cousin so the desk looks busy, you are minting. Stop.

#### Changing your mind

A thesis is a fallible experiment, not a personality. When the named kill prints, the thesis is dead. You do not rescue it with a cousin story. You do not keep it because the title was good. You write the death in the notebook, in Nathan’s words if he spoke, in a dated note if the print did the work. **[sourced]** / **[shop]**

Changing your mind is a skill. Drifting is not. Drifting is when the claim slides and nobody writes a new version. If the situation changed, write a new draft and kill the old one. Do not edit a kept thesis in place so the history disappears. **[sourced]** / **[ours]**

Macro still does not mint the replacement as live. Nathan keeps the new one too.

#### Knowledge-base first

Later drafts start in the notebook, then the live theses, then the Board. A bot that starts from this morning’s tape and “finds” a thesis is running the daily pack, or worse, minting from a tape. That is a different file, and it is a failure mode of this one. **[shop]**

#### Reflexivity is an amplifier, not a situation

Price can change the thing it is supposed to reflect. Funding can look like demand. A scare in the shares can change what gets built. That loop can sit *on* a situation as a warning or a boost. It is not itself a theme. **[sourced]**

If the only sentence you have is “it will feed on itself,” you have a slogan. Name the situation first. Then say whether the loop is currently interrupting or reinforcing it.

#### Structured disagreement, used as a gate

A good shop does not protect a draft from the case against it. Before you offer the draft, write the strongest honest objection in one paragraph — the Board item that fights it, the analog that does not fit, the “already in the price” line. If you cannot write that paragraph, you have not looked. **[sourced]**

This is not a score. It is a paragraph. Nathan reads it.

## 4. The conversion process

Same order every time this conversion is run. The early steps are the ones people skip in order to get to a title, so they are numbered.

Sitting after any step is a legal output. A blank pass that skipped to a title is not sitting. It is a skipped pass.

### Step 0 — Read the notebook first

On the first-ever conversion, the notebook may be empty. Say so. On every later conversion, this step is mandatory. **[shop]**

Read, in his words:

- what he kept, and why
- what he dropped, and why
- what he parked or sat on
- any sentence of his that says “this is not a thesis” or “this sits on TH-__”

Do not paraphrase his reason into a smarter reason. If you need to recall a drop, quote him.

If tonight’s Board is only reprinting a situation he already dropped, and nothing new answers his reason, stop. Write sit, with the notebook line cited.

If tonight’s Board is only reprinting a situation he already kept, do not draft a cousin. Sit the new print on the kept theme (step 3).

### Step 1 — Read the cooked Board

Read all of it. Situations, open calls, observations, housed stories, fresh prints.

For each item, mark one job, in words, not scores:

- **already a live theme** — this is evidence or a print on something kept
- **candidate situation** — might be one thesis if the gates pass
- **story / observation / fight** — stays on the Board
- **neighbor** — near a candidate, not the same kill
- **noise** — a tape wiggle, a slogan, a reprint

Do not draft yet. Do not grab yet.

A fight on the Board (“everyone agrees the problem; nobody agrees the path”) is not a parent theme. It stays a fight until the world picks. **[shop]**

An open call can be evidence for a candidate. It is not a thesis by itself.

### Step 2 — Grab only if needed

Grab means: go get a fact that would change whether you can write one situation, name a kill, or honestly say priced / not checked.

You may grab when:

- the situation is almost writable and one dated fact is missing
- the kill cannot be named without knowing whether a print already happened
- “priced” cannot even be called “not checked” because you have not looked at the one series the situation itself named

You may not grab:

- a tour of the daily pack “in case something is there”
- a name, a ticker, or a company story
- a second story to rescue a weak situation
- enough tape to retrofit a move that already happened

If after a short grab you still cannot write one situation, stop. Sit. The Board stays the Board. **[shop]** / **[ours]**

This step is not the daily tape pack. If you need the pack, you are in the other file.

### Step 3 — Associate, resonate, correlate

Run the four seeing skills on the candidate pile only.

**Associate.** For each candidate, name the other field that would have to update if the force is real. If that field is already a Board card with the same kill, it may join. If it is a different kill, it is a neighbor. Write neighbors down so you do not “link” them later in the title.

**Resonate.** Check the notebook, the live theses, and (only if needed) a mechanism-analog. If this is a kept theme in new clothes, it is a print, not a draft. If this is a dropped theme and his reason still holds, sit.

**Correlate.** Ask what already moves with this. Agreement with a kept theme → sit the print on the theme. Agreement with a popular narrative and no remaining room → priced warning, not a boost. Tape-correlation with no shared force → junk. Do not mint.

**Link.** Only join nodes that share force and kill. Write the join in a situation sentence, not in a title. If the sentence needs “and also” more than once, split or sit.

Output of this step is a short list: **join on theme X**, **draft this situation**, or **stay on the Board**. Most items should stay on the Board.

### Step 4 — Write one situation, or stop

For each “draft this situation” item, write the situation in ordinary sentences.

Required:

- the force (one sentence)
- which Board ids feed it, and why they are the same situation
- which Board ids were *not* included, and why (neighbors, fights, stories)
- what it is not (the obvious wrong reading: a country-invasion story, a crypto-price story, a “the whole stack” story)

If you cannot write “what it is not,” you do not have a situation. You have a mood. Stop. **[ours]**

No ticker. No expression. No hold.

If nothing on the list survives this step, the conversion’s output is sit. That is a completed pass.

### Step 5 — Invert

Write the kill. A world print, dated if the Board already gave you a date.

Write what is not a kill.

Write the strongest honest objection in one paragraph (the fighting Board item, the analog that does not fit, the chance this is already the consensus).

If inversion dissolves the situation, do not offer the draft. Leave the cards on the Board. Note the dissolve in the working notes, not as a dropped thesis — Nathan did not drop it, because you never offered it. **[ours]**

### Step 6 — Clock

Tag the draft: `now`, `6–12 months`, or the dated window you can stand behind.

Say what will be visible by then if the situation is true.

Do not invent a second draft that is the same situation at a different clock.

### Step 7 — Priced, or not checked

Write what the world already assumes, in ordinary sentences, **or** write `not checked`.

If the Board already says a scare ran in the shares, that belongs here. If default expressions look crowded, say that as a world-level priced warning, still without a ticker. If you have not checked, say not checked. **[shop]**

A draft may be offered with `not checked`. It may not be offered with a fake priced line.

### Step 8 — Draft for Nathan, never auto-keep

Offer the draft in the shape in section 6.

Then stop.

Do not assign a live `TH-*` id. Do not move it onto Theses as kept. Do not write a hold. Do not spawn a name. Do not “keep it warm” by parking a half-thesis in the Theses column.

Nathan and the Architect talk. Nathan keeps, drops, parks, or sits. Macro writes down what he said, in his words, in the notebook (section 5).

If Nathan does not speak, the draft stays a draft. Silence is not a keep. **[shop]**

## 5. How the notebook is used

The notebook is Macro’s memory of *Nathan’s* judgment. It is not a second Board. It is not a source of live theses.

### 5.1 What it stores

Each entry, dated:

- **Draft offered** — one-line situation, or “sit, nothing offered”
- **Nathan’s words** — kept / dropped / parked / sit, quoted
- **His reason** — quoted or plainly restated only if he asked you to tidy the sentence
- **Join instructions** — e.g. “this print sits on the plants theme”; “this is not a thesis”
- **Open fights he left on the Board** — so a later bot does not “resolve” them

Write his no as carefully as his yes. A shop that only journals keeps will re-pitch every drop forever.

### 5.2 How a later draft reads it

Before step 1:

1. If he kept a theme, tonight’s agreeing print joins that theme. You may refresh the evidence line on a *draft update* for him to accept. You do not mint a new id.
2. If he dropped a theme, you need a new fact that answers his reason. A new adjective is not a new fact.
3. If he said “not a thesis,” that sentence is a shop fact. It outranks your association skill.
4. If he sat, you may offer again only if the Board changed.

The notebook can stop a mint. It cannot authorize one. Macro does not mint a live thesis from a notebook entry, including from a keep. The keep already happened; you do not keep it again. **[shop]**

### 5.3 What the notebook is not

- Not a place to store Macro’s unused slogans
- Not a correlation map
- Not a list of tickers
- Not a substitute for the Board
- Not a license to edit a kept thesis so the old claim disappears

Where the notebook file lives is **[open]**. Until Nathan says otherwise, Macro keeps it as `docs/thesis-notebook.md`, append-only, his words first. If that file does not exist yet, say “notebook empty” at step 0 and continue. Do not invent past keeps to fill it.

## 6. What a draft writes

Ordinary sentences. No manager names. No unexplained labels. No ticker soup. A name appears only if the sentence cannot be true without a name you can stand behind, and even then this conversion prefers the generic (the foundry, the rail, the magnet) over a ticker. **[shop]**

**On a thesis draft (offered, not kept):**

- **Situation** — one force, in ordinary language
- **What it is not**
- **From the Board** — ids that feed it, and why they are the same situation
- **Left on the Board** — ids that stay stories, fights, neighbors, or prints on another theme
- **Clock** — tag, plus the testable window in words
- **Kill** — the world print that dies it
- **Not a kill** — the noise that will try
- **Priced** — what is already assumed, or `not checked`
- **Objection** — the strongest honest case against
- **Notebook** — any prior keep/drop this draft is answering
- **Disposition asked of Nathan** — keep / drop / park / sit / join to an existing theme

**On a sit (also a real output):**

- why nothing was a thesis tonight (notebook stop, no shared kill, still a fight, only a story, correlation without a situation, priced not checked and the situation too thin to offer)
- which prints were joined to a kept theme instead of drafted

**On a join-to-theme note (not a new draft):**

- the kept theme, by the id Nathan already kept
- the Board item that agrees
- the sentence: this sits on the theme; it is not a new card for its own sake

After Nathan keeps, the live thesis card may carry: claim, from-board, clock, kill, priced, and a desk line. Macro still does not write the hold. Micro does that later. **[shop]**

## 7. Failure modes we have already seen

Written down so they can be checked for rather than discovered. House patterns are method, not a live market claim in this document.

### 7.1 Minting from a tape

A scare, a bounce, or a quiet month in the shares is a tape event. It can be evidence that the *price* moved while the *situation* did not. That is a priced line, or a not-a-kill line, on a theme. It is not a new theme.

The house pattern: a crash month in the shares while the plants did not move was read as a price event, not a factory event. The call that the bottleneck still holds is evidence for the plants theme, not a second thesis. **[shop]**

Guard: step 3, correlate; step 7, priced. If the only new thing is a tape, join or sit. Do not draft.

### 7.2 Minting from a story

A housed story, a podcast, a founder’s line, a Narrative card — true or not — is still a story. Renaming it a thesis skips invert, clock, and priced.

Guard: section 2. Copying a Narrative card is a standing prohibition. If the draft’s sentences are the story’s sentences with a new header, throw the draft away.

### 7.3 Collage instead of one situation

Linking every Board card that shares a newspaper word into one “stack” thesis. Plants plus circular funding plus a payments rail plus a China input, because they all appear in the same week, is a collage.

The house pattern: several plant cards became one theme. The funding-exhausted card sat next to an open call and was *not* minted as a fourth thesis. The power fight stayed a fight. The payments card was its own situation or nothing. **[shop]**

Guard: same force, same kill. “And also” more than once → split or sit.

### 7.4 Easy-money slogan

Forcing every draft through easy/tight money, or using “liquidity” as the bot’s personality. A dated funding fact already on the Board can be part of a situation. A slogan cannot.

Guard: section 0, prohibition 2. If the force sentence is only about money being easy or tight, rewrite or sit.

### 7.5 Linking nodes that are not the same situation

Association and “create narratives” used as a license to leap. A title that joins what the kill cannot join.

Guard: section 3.1, linking rule. If card A and card B die on different prints, they are two situations.

### 7.6 Other failures this conversion will try

- **Auto-keep.** Draft moved to live Theses without Nathan. Guard: step 8.
- **Minting from the notebook.** A past keep reused as a new live id, or a past drop ignored. Guard: step 0 and section 5.2.
- **Minting a hold.** Expression written because the draft felt strong. Guard: section 2.4.
- **A print that agrees becomes its own card.** Guard: step 3, join-to-theme.
- **Clock as a column.** Two theses that are one situation at two speeds. Guard: step 6.
- **Fake priced line.** Skew invented so the draft looks finished. Guard: step 7, “not checked” is legal.
- **Grab as a fishing trip.** Daily pack rerun inside this conversion. Guard: step 2.
- **Name-drop or ticker soup.** Guard: section 0 and section 6.
- **Rescuing a dead thesis** with a cousin story. Guard: changing-your-mind in 3.2.
- **Resolving a fight** the Board still marks as a fight. Guard: step 1.

## 8. Against fake precision

- No composite “thesis score.” The situation is written, or it is not.
- No numeric trigger unless Nathan sets it.
- No invented current market claims. If it is not on the Board and you did not grab it with a date, you do not know it.
- No invented tickers. A generic (“the foundry,” “the rail”) is better than a name you cannot stand behind.
- Words for disposition — keep, drop, park, sit, join. “Unclear” is allowed and often correct.
- Where a number is genuinely countable — a dated call, a rent, a utilization print — use it. That is the opposite of this failure.
