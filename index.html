15 board card
AI chip factories are still dramatically under-built
The plants that make the chips were not built fast enough for the demand now hitting them. That shortage, not a China-invasion story, is still the brake. New tapes this week did not kill it. They keep running into the same wall.
Force: they did not build enough plants. Supply and demand, not political theater.

7 board card
In July the market panicked. The factories did not.
AI stock prices had a crash month that felt like 2022. Gavin Baker went looking for a real-world reason and could not find one. Chips were getting easier to get, rental prices were up, memory was up, and people were using more tokens, not less. The scare was in the price of the shares. It was not in the plants.
Force: the share price and the factory can point opposite ways. Read both.
From: Gavin Baker, Invest Like the Best, 4 Aug. MSM: FT Alphaville, BBC, and the FSB ran the July “AI bubble” scare.

16 board card
Compute is getting dearer, and old cards still earn
Useful intelligence is getting more expensive, not cheaper. An H100 is worth more today than three years ago. When a model got ten times cheaper, people used it thirteen times more. A six-year write-off looks wrong because the cards stay in the money long after the accountant is done with them.
Force: more demand for the same scarce machines, and the machines last.

6 board card
Ordinary funding may be exhausted. That is why they are lending to themselves.
The plants and the labs need more cash than earnings, banks, and ordinary borrowing can cover. That claim still needs proof. If it is true, they have to invent other money: chip sellers lending to their own buyers, Nvidia reaching into insurance and pension money, the same dollars going around the same few companies and getting counted as revenue. Circular funding and the debt pile are what you do when the normal tap is dry. The BIS has already said the biggest cloud companies are spending faster than they earn. That is a start, not a close.
Force: if ordinary money is exhausted, the stack starts lending to itself. Prove the exhaustion, not the weirdness.
From: the board frame. All-In (Gurley circular). Stratechery 11 Aug Nvidia’s risky business. Gavin Baker on credit wrappers. BIS on hyperscaler spend vs cash.

11 board card fight
Power is the problem. Nobody agrees what happens next.
Everyone serious now says intelligence will hit a wall made of electricity. That part is shared. The fight is about the future. One camp says China will add far more power than America next year, so energy stays the limit. Another camp says you can build power, and the real 2030 bind is the rarest machines that make the chips. A third camp says put the plants in space, because on the ground you cannot get a permit. These cannot all be right about what happens next.
Force: the problem is agreed. The path and the fix are not. The world will pick by what actually fails first.
From: Vishria 11 Aug. Dylan Patel 13 Mar. Dwarkesh Elon 5 Feb. Stratechery SpaceX IPO.

10 board card
China is locking the ugly supply chains
China stopped exporting research monkeys. Prices jumped, US labs ran short. It already refines most rare earths and is moving to own the robots that run the mines, not just the rocks. In the Iran oil shock it quietly cut imports from stockpiles the West cannot see. The FCC just swept robot vacuums and humanoids onto a national-security import list. Each of these is an input the West did not build a second source for.
Force: whoever owns the unglamorous input can turn it off.
From: ChinaTalk 14 Jul–13 Aug (monkeys, minerals, oil, FCC robots).

P1 prediction
The chip factories will still be the bottleneck at Christmas, even if the stocks crash again
The plants that make AI chips were not built fast enough. That is why computers for intelligence are expensive, and why a three-year-old chip still earns money. In July the newspapers and the stock market had a panic. A fund manager went looking for a factory reason and could not find one. The scare was in the share price. It was not in the factories.
By 31 December 2026 that is still true. The wait for a new chip, and the price to rent one, will still be tight or tighter.
Wrong if: the wait gets easy and rents collapse, while everyone is still talking about AI. Evidence for TH-01. Not a second thesis.
How sure: 4/5 · How big if true: 5/5 · Call by 31 Dec 2026 · open
P2 prediction
If ordinary money is exhausted, one of these alternative deals will break in public within a year
If the usual funding is exhausted, companies are no longer just paying cash. Chip sellers are lending to their own buyers. Nvidia is reaching into insurance money and pension money. The same dollars go around the same few companies and get counted as revenue. That can look like a boom on paper while the cash never came from a real customer.
Before 14 August 2027, one of these loans or insurance deals will blow up, get rewritten, or show up in a newspaper with a real company name on it. A podcast complaining about circular money is not enough.
Wrong if: a year from now the plants are still being paid with ordinary cash, and the strange paper is gone. Sits next to board card 6. Not minted as a thesis this walk.
How sure: 3/5 · How big if true: 5/5 · Call by 14 Aug 2027 · open
P3 prediction
OpenAI and Anthropic will still take the money. Cheap models will take the usage.
There are two markets now, and they are not the same. The two best labs can still charge for the model itself. Everyone a year behind can only charge for computers and consulting. Cheap and Chinese open models already do most of the everyday work. Money and usage have split.
A year from now that split is still there. The expensive labs still collect most of the dollars. The cheap models still do most of the work.
Wrong if: a cheap model starts collecting the dollars too, or the expensive labs lose the dollar pile. Priced / capture question. Not a new desk item.
How sure: 4/5 · How big if true: 4/5 · Call by 14 Aug 2027 · open
OB-01 observation
China catching up in trailing-edge robotics semiconductors
More than 300 Chinese auto and industrial chip suppliers are on the trailing-edge nodes that robots actually use. NXP and Infineon share is fading in those sockets. This is not a leading-edge TSMC story.
Force: the chips inside robots are older nodes, and China is filling that shelf.
From: kept observation. SemiAnalysis, 11 Mar 2025.
